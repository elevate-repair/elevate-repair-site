What to weigh before you decide

  • What failed. A drain pump, inlet valve, door latch, heating element or gasket is one part. The pump, motor and control board failing together is a different situation.
  • Leaks. A leak from a gasket, hose or door seal is worth repairing promptly rather than living with; it is normally a single-part fix.
  • The tub and frame. A tub or frame that is cracked or rusted through cannot be fixed with a part swap, and that is when replacing makes more sense.
  • Fit. A built-in unit is already fitted to your cabinets and plumbing. A replacement has to match the opening and the connections.
  • The quote against the price of a comparable new dishwasher. We give you the exact repair price after diagnosis so the comparison is real.

Usually worth diagnosing and repairing

These are the everyday dishwasher repairs: one failed part, replaced on site, with the dishwasher staying in its cabinet.

  • A dishwasher that won't drain or holds standing water: a drain pump, a drain hose, an air gap or check valve, or a food chopper.
  • A dishwasher leaking: a door gasket or tub seal, a float switch or a general leak repair.
  • A dishwasher not cleaning: a circulation pump or wash motor, a spray arm or a diverter motor.
  • A dishwasher not drying: a heating element, or a thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat.
  • A dishwasher that won't start: a door latch or safety switch, an electronic control board or a touchpad board.
  • A dishwasher making noise, or a rack adjuster, tine row clip or detergent dispenser.
  • A built-in unit already fitted to your cabinets and plumbing.

When replacement may make more sense

  • The tub or frame is cracked or rusted through.
  • The pump, motor and control board are failing together.
  • The repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new dishwasher.

Parts for your model that are discontinued or on a long backorder also tip the decision toward replacing. We tell you which case you are in after the diagnosis, before anything is quoted or done.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a leaking dishwasher worth repairing?

Usually yes, and promptly. A leak normally comes from a door gasket or tub seal, a hose or a float switch, which is a single-part repair on a built-in unit already fitted to your cabinets and plumbing. We quote the exact repair after diagnosis, before any work begins.

My dishwasher won't drain. Repair or replace?

Repair, in most cases. Standing water usually means a drain pump, a drain hose, an air gap or check valve, or a food chopper, each a single part on an otherwise sound dishwasher.

When is a dishwasher not worth repairing?

When the tub or frame is cracked or rusted through, when the pump, motor and control board are failing together, or when the repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new dishwasher. We tell you which case you are in after the diagnosis.
