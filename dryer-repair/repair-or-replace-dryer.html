Repair or Replace a Dryer?

The short answer

Diagnose first. A dryer that runs without heat, will not start or squeals usually has one failed part in its heating circuit or drive, and one part on a dryer whose drum, motor and controls are sound is normally worth repairing.

What to weigh before you decide

  • What failed. A thermostat, thermal fuse, heating element, belt, roller or idler pulley is one part. The drum, motor and controls failing together is a different situation.
  • Airflow. Long drying times and overheating are often an airflow problem, which a vent cleaning and a worn-part replacement normally solve.
  • Installation. A stacked or laundry-closet unit would be awkward to swap out, which tips a single-part fault toward repair.
  • The quote against the price of a comparable new dryer. We give you the exact repair price after diagnosis so the comparison is real.
  • Parts availability for your model. Discontinued parts or a long backorder change the answer.

Usually worth diagnosing and repairing

These are the everyday dryer repairs: one failed part, replaced on site, with the dryer staying where it stands.

  • A dryer not heating: a heating element on an electric dryer, a thermal fuse, or a high-limit or cycling thermostat.
  • A dryer that won't start: a thermal fuse, a door switch, or a power cord or terminal block.
  • A dryer making noise: drum rollers, an idler pulley, a blower wheel or a drum felt seal.
  • A dryer that takes too long or is overheating: an internal exhaust vent cleaning, a moisture sensor bar or a blower wheel.
  • A dryer that won't tumble: a drive belt, an idler pulley or a drive motor.
  • A stacked or laundry-closet unit that would be awkward to swap out.

When replacement may make more sense

  • The drum, motor and controls are failing together.
  • The repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new dryer.
  • Parts for your model are discontinued or on a long backorder.

A dryer with poor airflow is not a replacement case on its own: a vent cleaning and a worn-part replacement normally solve it, and we check the airflow as part of the diagnosis.

A note on gas models. The decision works the same way for a gas or an electric dryer: one failed part on a sound machine is normally worth repairing, and the drum, belt, rollers, idler pulley, blower and airflow parts are the same on both. The heating parts differ, so tell us which type you have when you book. For gas dryers: if you smell natural gas near the dryer at any point, do not attempt to operate the machine or test components. Leave the area, avoid operating any electrical switches, and contact your gas utility provider's emergency line from outside the building.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a dryer that runs but does not heat worth repairing?

Usually yes. A dryer that tumbles without heat most often has one failed part in its heating circuit: a heating element on an electric dryer, a thermal fuse, or a high-limit or cycling thermostat. We replace the part on site and quote the exact repair after diagnosis, before any work begins.

My dryer squeaks or thumps. Repair or replace?

Repair, in most cases. Noise usually comes from drum rollers, an idler pulley, a blower wheel or a drum felt seal, each a single wear part on a dryer whose drum, motor and controls are sound.

Does a stacked or laundry-closet dryer change the decision?

Yes. A stacked or laundry-closet unit would be awkward to swap out, so a single failed part on one of those is normally worth repairing where it stands.
