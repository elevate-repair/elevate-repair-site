What to weigh before you decide

  • What failed. One part (a defrost heater or thermostat, a fan motor, a start relay, a door gasket or a water valve) is the normal case. Several major components at once is not.
  • Food at risk. A refrigerator that has stopped cooling needs a decision today. Book the diagnosis, then decide with the quote in hand instead of guessing.
  • Fit. Built-in, counter-depth and French-door models are matched to a kitchen cutout and a water line. A replacement has to fit the same space.
  • The quote against the price of a comparable new refrigerator. We give you the exact repair price after diagnosis so the comparison is real.
  • Parts availability for your model. Discontinued parts or a long backorder change the answer.

Usually worth diagnosing and repairing

These are the everyday refrigerator repairs: one failed part, replaced on site, with the refrigerator staying where it is.

  • A refrigerator not cooling while the compressor runs: usually a defrost heater or defrost thermostat, an evaporator or condenser fan motor, a damper or a temperature sensor.
  • A failed compressor start relay or capacitor on an otherwise sound refrigerator.
  • A fridge leaking water or water under the crisper drawers: a defrost drain tube, a water inlet valve or a filter housing.
  • An ice maker not making ice or a water dispenser not working on a refrigerator that otherwise works.
  • A freezer not freezing while the fresh-food side holds temperature.
  • A fridge making noise from a fan motor, or a door gasket, hinge, crisper track or leveling problem.
  • A built-in, counter-depth or French-door model that fits your kitchen cutout.

When replacement may make more sense

  • Several major components are failing at the same time.
  • The repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new refrigerator.
  • Parts for your model are discontinued or on a long backorder.

If the diagnosis points to the sealed system (the compressor and refrigerant circuit), we explain what we found and what your options are before anything is quoted or done. Either way, you decide with the facts in front of you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth repairing a refrigerator that is not cooling?

Usually yes. A refrigerator that runs but does not cool most often has one failed part: a defrost heater or defrost thermostat, an evaporator or condenser fan motor, a start relay, a damper or a temperature sensor. We replace the part on site and quote the exact repair after diagnosis, before any work begins. If several major components are failing at the same time, we tell you that instead.

Does a built-in, counter-depth or French-door refrigerator change the decision?

Yes. Those models are matched to a kitchen cutout and a water line, so a replacement has to fit the same space and finish. A single failed part on a built-in, counter-depth or French-door refrigerator is normally worth repairing.

What if only the freezer or the ice maker is the problem?

A freezer compartment that will not freeze or an ice maker that has stopped on a refrigerator that otherwise works is normally a repair, not a replacement. See our freezer repair and ice maker repair pages for those faults.
