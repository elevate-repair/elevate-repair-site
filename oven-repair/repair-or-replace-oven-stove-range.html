The short answer

Diagnose first. An oven that will not heat, a burner that stays cold or a door that will not close usually has one failed part behind it, and one part on an otherwise sound wall oven, range or cooktop is normally worth repairing.

Damage to the oven cavity or frame is the one fault that points to replacing.

What to weigh before you decide

  • What failed. A bake or broil element, temperature sensor, burner switch, door hinge or gasket is one part. The control board, elements and sensors failing together is a different situation.
  • Which type you have. A built-in wall oven, slide-in range or cooktop is fitted to your cabinets, which tips a single-part fault toward repair. A freestanding range is simpler to replace.
  • The cavity and frame. An oven cavity or frame that is damaged beyond a part swap is when replacing makes more sense.
  • The quote against the price of a comparable new range or wall oven. We give you the exact repair price after diagnosis so the comparison is real.
  • Parts availability for your model. Discontinued parts or a long backorder change the answer.

Usually worth diagnosing and repairing

These are the everyday oven, stove and range repairs: one failed part, replaced on site, with the appliance staying in its cutout.

  • An oven not heating: a bake or broil element, an oven temperature sensor or a control board.
  • An oven temperature that is inaccurate: an oven temperature sensor or a control board.
  • A burner not working on an electric cooktop: a coil burner, a glass-cooktop radiant element or a burner control switch.
  • An oven door that won't close: a door hinge, a door gasket or door glass.
  • An oven that won't turn off or a self-clean cycle that no longer runs on an otherwise sound oven.
  • A convection or cooling fan motor, an indicator light or switch, a drip bowl or a terminal block.
  • A built-in wall oven or slide-in range fitted to your cabinets.

When replacement may make more sense

  • The oven cavity or frame is damaged beyond a part swap.
  • The control board, elements and sensors are failing together.
  • The repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new range.

Parts for your model that are discontinued or on a long backorder also tip the decision toward replacing. We tell you which case you are in after the diagnosis, before anything is quoted or done.

A note on gas models. The decision works the same way for a gas, electric or dual-fuel model: one failed part on a sound oven or range is normally worth repairing, and the door, hinge, gasket, glass and most controls are the same on both. The burner and ignition parts differ, so tell us which type you have when you book. If you smell gas when attempting to use the oven, or at any time near the appliance, do not operate the oven, do not operate any electrical switches, and do not attempt to light the oven manually. Leave the area, ventilate without using fans, and contact your gas utility's emergency line from outside the building.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an oven that won't heat worth repairing?

Usually yes. An oven that will not heat most often has one failed part: a bake or broil element, an oven temperature sensor or a control board. We replace the part on site and quote the exact repair after diagnosis, before any work begins.

Should I repair or replace a built-in wall oven?

A built-in wall oven is fitted to a cabinet cutout, so a single failed part on an otherwise sound unit is normally worth repairing. Replacing it means matching the cutout and the connection. We give you the exact repair price after diagnosis so you can compare it with the price of a comparable new wall oven.

One burner on my cooktop stopped working. Repair or replace?

Repair, in most cases. On an electric cooktop a cold burner usually means a coil burner, a glass-cooktop radiant element or a burner control switch, each a single part. Tell us whether your cooktop is gas or electric when you book.

When should I replace a range instead of repairing it?

When the oven cavity or frame is damaged beyond a part swap, when the control board, elements and sensors are failing together, or when the repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new range. We tell you which case you are in after the diagnosis.
