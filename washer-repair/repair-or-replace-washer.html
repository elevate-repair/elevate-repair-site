The short answer

Diagnose first. A washer that leaks, will not drain or will not spin normally has one failed part behind it, and that is usually a one-visit fix on a machine whose tub, motor and drum are sound.

What to weigh before you decide

  • What failed. A lid switch or door lock, drain pump, inlet valve, belt or shock absorbers is one part. The tub bearing, motor and control board failing together is a different situation.
  • Which repair group it falls into. Controls & sensors and Parts & mechanical repairs are the everyday fixes. Major teardown repairs (tub bearing, spider arm, tub seal) mean the tub and drum come apart, and that is where we compare the quote with you.
  • The condition of the rest of the machine. A sound tub, motor and drum on a front-load or top-load washer make a single-part repair worthwhile.
  • The quote against the price of a comparable new washer. We give you the exact repair price after diagnosis so the comparison is real.
  • Parts availability for your model. Discontinued parts or a long backorder change the answer.

Usually worth diagnosing and repairing

These are the everyday washer repairs: one failed part, replaced on site, normally in a single visit.

  • A washer leaking water: a front-load door boot gasket, a drain hose or a water inlet valve.
  • A washer that won't drain: a drain pump or a coin trap and drain filter.
  • A washer that won't spin or won't start: a lid switch or door lock, a motor coupling, a drive belt, a shift actuator or a clutch assembly.
  • A washer not filling: a water inlet valve or a water level (pressure) switch.
  • A washer shaking or vibrating: shock absorbers on a front-load or suspension rods on a top-load machine.
  • A timer, main control board or touchpad board on a machine whose tub, motor and drum are sound.

When replacement may make more sense

  • The tub bearing, motor and control board are failing together.
  • The repair quote comes close to the price of a comparable new washer.
  • Parts for your model are discontinued or on a long backorder.

The Major teardown repairs group (tub bearing, spider arm, tub seal) is the decision point. The tub and drum have to come apart for those jobs, so when the diagnosis lands there we put the quote next to the price of a comparable new washer with you before anything is done.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a leaking washer worth repairing?

Usually yes. A leak normally comes from a front-load door boot gasket, a drain hose or a water inlet valve, which is a one-visit repair on a machine whose tub, motor and drum are sound. We quote the exact repair after diagnosis, before any work begins.

My washer won't drain or spin. Repair or replace?

Repair, in most cases. A washer that will not drain usually needs a drain pump or a cleared coin trap and drain filter. A washer that will not spin usually needs a lid switch or door lock, a motor coupling, a drive belt, a shift actuator or a clutch assembly. Each of those is a single part on an otherwise sound machine.

When does a teardown repair change the decision?

The Major teardown repairs group covers the tub bearing, the front-load spider arm and the tub seal. Those jobs need the tub and drum taken apart, so when the diagnosis lands there we compare the quote with the price of a comparable new washer with you before any work begins.
